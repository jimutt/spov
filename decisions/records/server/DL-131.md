## Context

The recorder bounds each frame (1 MiB) and each read of a sandbox (8 MiB), but nothing bounded their sum. Three things followed, each reachable by any code running in a sandbox, which every agent is:

- A frame naming a session id the span had not seen cost one read of up to 8 MiB, stored as the conversation's `info` and held in memory for the span. `MaxConversations` (32) bounds only the catch-up listing. 300 small frames held 314 MB, and every reader of the session's conversations loaded the blobs again.
- A stream of durable frames filled the shared store for as long as the sandbox lived.
- The verdict held up to 20,000 events whatever their size.

## Decision

This amends DL-017 and DL-018 (the recorder), DL-089 (the stored info) and DL-050 (what the verdict holds).

### Conversations

- **A session stores at most 800 conversations** (`MaxSessionConversations`). The recorder reads which ones the session holds at attach. A frame, or a listing entry, that names a new one past the limit is dropped before any read of the sandbox, and one note (`spov.recorder.conversations.limit`) is written to the session's log the first time, across spans and restarts. Frames of the conversations it holds are recorded as before.
- **A conversation's info over 128 KiB is not stored** (`MaxConversationInfoBytes`); the conversation is, with its title and parent. A transcript resume then builds the info from the row, as it does for a conversation that never had one.
- **`ListConversations` carries no info.** `ConversationInfos` reads it, for the transcript import alone. The recorder keeps a hash of the info it last wrote, not the info.

`MaxConversations`, the 32 a single catch-up reads, is unchanged.

### One session's storage

- **A session stores at most 1,000,000 events and messages, or 1 GiB of their text and raw frames** (`MaxSessionRows`; `history.max_session_mib`, default 1024, for the size). The setting applies to a hosted server and a local one.
- The recorder measures the session at attach (`ConversationStore.StoredSize`) and then counts what each write reports: the events a write inserted, at their size, and for messages the rows `PutMessages` added and the net change in bytes it made (`store.MessageWrite`), so a rewritten message counts its size difference and an unchanged one nothing. Postgres computes the net change in the upsert itself (`PutMessage` returns the new size and the size of the row it replaced); the memory store diffs the row it holds.
- When the count reaches a limit, the capture measures the store again, at most once per `RemeasureInterval` (one minute, on the recorder's injected clock), and in between believes the count. The count misses only what other writers add (spov's own markers), so a measure finds the session at or above what the count said; `StoredSize` is read once at attach and at most once a minute after that, never once per page or frame. The memory store answers `StoredSize` from its per-session indexes of events and messages, not a scan of every session.
- **At the limit the recording of that session stops.** The recorder writes `spov.recorder.stopped` with the reason and gives the session up, as it does after its redial budget; the transcript's warning shows the text. The sandbox is not stopped. A later process finds the session over its limit, sees the marker and adds none.
- The final read before a sandbox is terminated keeps the same limits.

A job whose recording stops is still followed on what spov writes itself. The storage limit stops only the recorder; spov's own markers (a person's send, an interrupt, a resume, an answer) are written as before. A person's message never reaches the log as an inbox frame once the recording has stopped, so the supervisor reads spov's record of the send in its place: `spov.session.steered` carries a `SteerRecord` (`{message, delivery, after}`) as its raw body, and a send after a `spov.recorder.stopped` marker that did not say `continue` takes the job over, moving it to `steered` at the send rather than at its delivery (DL-052). For the proposed-check rule (DL-050), such a send is a person writing. This applies after any stop marker, the redial give-up's and a panic's included, since none of them records the send; after a give-up a later process may record it too, and steering twice is no move. Otherwise the job runs to its wall clock or its sandbox's end, and is judged on what was stored.

### Readers of a session's history

No reader that a request, a supervisor pass or a timer reaches loads a session's whole history. `ConversationStore.RecentMessages` returns a session's (or one conversation's) newest messages, oldest first, at most a count and a size of text and raw frame, and says whether older ones were left out. In Postgres it is one query over the `messages_last` index, or the new `messages_conversation_tail` index for one conversation, whose window sum stops at the limit; a row past the size comes back without its text, so the bound is never fetched.

| Reader | Reads |
|---|---|
| `Supervisor.loopStoppedInMessages`, every pass of a running job | the newest `StopMarkerWindow` (20) messages of the job's own conversation (`store.Primary`), at most `StopMarkerWindowBytes` (16 MiB) |
| `Service.jobLoopAnnouncedStop`, on a resume | the same, through `jobs.LoopStoppedInRecent` |
| the verdict's `jobMessages`, once per job | the session's newest `MaxVerdictMessages` (20,000) messages, at most `MaxVerdictBytes` (256 MiB), as `jobEvents` keeps the newest events |
| `GET /api/v1/sessions/{ref}/messages` (`Server.transcript`) | the session's newest `MaxTranscriptMessages` (20,000) messages, at most `MaxTranscriptBytes` (64 MiB); older ones are left out, the body's `truncated` is true and a warning says so |

The transcript is one response, not a page: the page draws a window of its newest blocks (DL-070) and the CLI reads only conversation ids, so neither needs more, and both ignore a field they do not know. Only the transcript import of a resume (`Service.importConversations`, once per resume) reads every message, bounded by the session's limit (`TestOnlyTheTranscriptImportReadsEveryMessage`).

### The verdict

`jobEvents` holds at most 256 MiB of events (`MaxVerdictBytes`) as well as 20,000 of them, dropping the head as DL-050 does and always keeping the newest.

## Rationale

- 800 conversations and 128 KiB are far above what a session with many subagents produces, and bound the worst listing a reader can be made to load to the rows alone.
- Stopping the recording, not the sandbox, keeps a person's work alive when a limit was simply too low for them; the note tells them, and the setting raises it.
- Counting what each write reports keeps the count honest without a counter in the store. An average over a page would let a page of unchanged small messages and one new large one count at a page's fraction of its size, so a session could store about `MessagePageSize` times its limit. `octet_length` reads a stored value's size without fetching it, so a measure is a scan of row headers, and the interval keeps it to once a minute per capture however a sandbox replays its frames.
- Only the newest messages can hold the stop marker: the plugin's loop sends nothing after it. Reading 20 of the job's own conversation costs the same however long the session is.

## Consequences

- Deleting a hosted session's history after a retention period is not part of this. Hosted history is kept forever unless an operator opts in, and that is its own decision.
- The verdict judges gate 3 on at most 20,000 of the newest messages and 256 MiB of them. A run longer than that is judged on its tail, as its events are.
- A transcript past 20,000 messages or 64 MiB shows only its newest; the rest stays stored and a resume still imports it.
- A stop marker older than the newest 20 messages of the job's conversation is not recovered from the transcript; the event log and the backstop (DL-048) still end the job.
- A person's handover to a job whose recording stopped steers at the send, not at its delivery, so the job is `steered` a moment before the agent reads the message.
- The 800 and the 1 GiB are not yet checked against measured long sessions; `TestIntegrationMeasureLongConversations` is the tool.
