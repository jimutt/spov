## Context

DL-191 stores schedules with a `next_run_at`. Something must notice a due slot and create its job exactly once, across replicas and restarts, without a second way of creating jobs.

## Decision

- `Service.RunSchedules` runs as one more loop on the replica holding the reconciler lock (`serve.go`'s leader block, beside the job sweep; DL-004), every `ScheduleTick` (1 min). Each tick claims up to `MaxSchedulesPerTick` (50) active schedules with `next_run_at <= now`, oldest first (`ScheduleStore.ClaimDue`, `for update skip locked` on Postgres), and fires each.
- A fire's slot is the schedule's `next_run_at`. When the slot is more than `ScheduleCatchUp` (1 h) late, only the newest slot at or before now is fired and the ones between are recorded as missed in one history entry (DL-193); so a server that was down fires at most one catch-up per schedule.
- A fire creates the job through `Service.CreateJob` with the owner's principal, built from the owner's row as authentication would: a person only while the allowlist still covers them (`AllowlistSet.Covers`), a service account only while it is not disabled, never an admin principal. An owner no longer allowed is a failed fire (DL-193), and the schedule is paused by off-boarding anyway (DL-193).
- The job records where it came from: `store.Job.ScheduleID` and `ScheduledFor` (columns `schedule_id`, `scheduled_for`, migration with a unique index on the pair where `schedule_id` is set; the memory store refuses the same pair). A second fire of the same slot is `ErrConflict` and is treated as already fired, so a replica that lost the lock mid-fire, or a retried tick, never creates two jobs for one slot.
- After each fire, started or not, `next_run_at` moves to the next slot strictly after both the fired slot and now, in the schedule's zone, in the same transaction as its history entry.
- "Run now" (DL-194) fires the schedule at the current minute as its slot, through the same path and the same skip rules, and does not move `next_run_at`.

## Rationale

Creating through `CreateJob` keeps one path for every job, so prep (DL-047), quotas (DL-045), the wall clock (DL-049), the verdict (DL-050) and the job's page all apply unchanged. The unique (schedule, slot) pair makes a fire idempotent where it matters, in the store, rather than trusting the lock alone. One catch-up after downtime is what a person would want from a daily report; ten queued reports would not be.

## Consequences

- A schedule fires up to a minute after its slot.
- A fire depends on the leader replica: while no replica holds the lock, nothing fires, and the next holder catches up once.
