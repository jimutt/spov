## Context

Every job gets one model call on its goal before provisioning (DL-047). The session opens with the goal followed by the assessment, and the pass may propose a check that gate 1 then runs.

A goal written by a program is already the brief its author wants the agent to read. The assessment adds a second voice to it, the proposed check is one the caller did not choose, and the call costs time and tokens on every job of a pipeline.

## Decision

A job request gains `skip_prep` (a boolean, false by default), valid on every kind. `spov jobs create` takes it as `--no-prep`. The browser's form does not offer it.

With it set, `Supervisor.prepare` returns the job unchanged before any call: no model is asked, the session opens on the goal alone, `Assessment` stays empty and no check is proposed. A check the caller set is run as on any job.

The value is stored on the job (`jobs.skip_prep`), fixed at create, so a redelivered attempt skips again. A job's view shows it only when it is set.

## Rationale

- The pass exists for goals a person typed. A caller that says its goal needs none is the one who knows.
- A stored flag, rather than a rule about how the goal looks or who sent it, keeps the pass's behaviour predictable for everyone else.

## Consequences

- A job that skips the pass and sets no check has gate 1 unverified, as any job with no check has (DL-050).
- The startup note about no prep provider and the warning for a failed call (DL-047) are unchanged; a skipped job writes neither.
