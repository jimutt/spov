## Context

From OpenCode 2.3.0 a subagent always runs in the background. Measured at 2.3.0 with the local probe (S-SA1): the `subagent` call succeeds at once with the child's session id, the parent's turn ends (`session.execution.succeeded`) while the child is still working, and when the child goes idle its report is enqueued in the parent's inbox as a `synthetic` item that starts a new parent turn. The call no longer takes a `background` input that changes this.

Two things follow for a job. The plugin continues the loop on every `session.execution.succeeded` of the job's session (DL-039), so a parent that has nothing to do but wait would be continued again and again, each turn billed, until the child reports. And the agent can call the completion tool (DL-048) while a child is still working.

## Decision

### Completion and delivery do not wait

A declared completion ends the run and starts delivery as before, whatever subagents, background shell commands or other background work are still running in the sandbox. The completion tool never refuses or defers because of them, and delivery ends the sandbox under them. Nothing in the supervisor reads whether a child is running.

The agent is told instead. The completion tool's description, the continuation text and the generated `/workspace/AGENTS.md` say that subagents run in the background and report back on their own, that work a subagent has not reported is lost when the job completes, and that the agent should wait for the reports it needs before calling the tool. This is guidance, as the "last action" sentence is. The description and the continuation carry one sentence, `opencode.SubagentNote`; a job's `AGENTS.md` has a "Subagents" section (`writeSubagentSection`), which an interactive session's does not.

### The loop holds its continuation while a subagent of the job runs

The plugin tracks the job session's direct children: a `session.created` whose parent is the job's session adds a child, and the child's execution frames say whether it has a turn in flight (`session.execution.started`, then `succeeded`, `failed` or `interrupted`). A child counts as having a turn in flight from its `session.created`, which arrives as its first turn starts. Children of children are not tracked; their parent reports for them.

The plugin can subscribe after the job's opening turn has begun, and then misses that turn's first frames: measured with the real plugin at the pin, the first session it heard of was the subagent's, whose prompt is a `user` item in the child's own inbox. A session seen created with a parent is therefore never taken for the job's session, and a child created while the job's session is not yet known names it (its parent). This refines DL-054's "learned from the first execution event", and keeps a child's prompt from reading as a person's takeover (DL-052).

When the job session's turn ends and at least one tracked child has a turn in flight, the plugin sends no continuation and records `continue.waiting` with the number of running children. The child's report starts the next turn by itself, and that turn's end is handled as any other.

The hold is bounded. `SubagentWait` (`opencode.SubagentWait`, rendered into the plugin's config) is 10 minutes, counted from the turn end that started the hold. If no turn of the job's session has started by then, the plugin sends the ordinary continuation with one added sentence saying how many subagents are still running (`subagentWaitNote`), and the next turn end may hold again. A paused or stopped loop, a held loop (DL-052) and a failed turn's retry (DL-048) are unchanged, and the hold never delays a stop marker.

A child's report is a `synthetic` item, so it never takes the job over (DL-052) and never counts toward `inbox.pending`.

## Rationale

A delivered job has had its deliverable collected and judged; nothing a child does afterwards can reach it, and waiting would let an orphaned subagent or a server left running hold the job and its billing until the wall clock. Whether the work is done is the agent's call (DL-048), so the remedy for a premature declaration is telling the agent, not the supervisor second-guessing it.

The hold exists only to stop paying for turns in which the parent can do nothing but wait. It is bounded so that a child that never goes idle cannot silence the loop; the wall clock (DL-049) still bounds the job either way.

## Consequences

- A job that fans work out to subagents costs no parent turns while it waits, up to ten minutes at a time.
- An agent that declares early loses its subagents' unreported work, and the verdict judges what was delivered.
- The plugin reads `session.created`'s parent and the execution frames of sessions other than its own. Spike S-SA3 measures both with a plugin loaded, and runs the hold end to end with the real plugin, on an OpenCode bump: `SPOV_PROBE=1 go test -tags integration ./internal/server/ -run SSA3 -v` (local, fake model, no key).

## Measurements

S-SA3 at 2.3.0, on the plugin's own subscription: a child's `session.created` carries `data.parentID` (the job's session) and `data.sessionID`; its `session.execution.started` arrives before the parent's `session.execution.succeeded`; the child's `session.execution.succeeded` arrives before its report is enqueued in the parent's inbox (`type: synthetic`, `metadata.source: "subagent"`), which starts the parent's next turn. With the real plugin the job's requests ran: the call to `subagent`, the turn's end with no continuation (`continue.waiting`, one running), the turn on the report, then an ordinary continuation and the completion. Before the hold, the same run was held as a person's takeover by the child's prompt.
