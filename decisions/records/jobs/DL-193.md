## Context

A fire can find the schedule's previous job still running, the owner at their concurrent job limit (DL-045), or a create that is refused (a retired model, a deleted template or credential, an owner no longer allowed). Agreed with the user: skip rather than queue, record why, and pause a schedule whose creates keep failing; skips do not count as failures.

## Decision

- Before creating, a fire skips its slot when a job this schedule created is still unfinished (`store.JobState.Counts`, the job quota's own set), recorded as `skipped: previous run still running` with that job's id.
- A create refused with `ErrQuotaExceeded` is recorded as `skipped: at the job limit`, naming the limit's numbers as the refusal does.
- Any other refusal or error is a failed fire, recorded with the refusal's text through `clientText` (never a store error's detail, DL-135). `consecutive_failures` counts them and is reset by any fire that starts a job; at `MaxFailedFires` (3) the schedule is paused with `paused_reason` "paused after 3 failed runs in a row: <the last failure>". A skip leaves the count as it is.
- A run now (DL-194) that is skipped writes no history, so repeated presses never push the slots' runs out of it, and one that fails is answered to the person and counts toward no pause.
- Each fire writes one history entry (`store.ScheduleFire`: slot, fired at, outcome `started`, `skipped`, `failed` or `missed`, reason, job id), at most `MaxScheduleHistory` (50) per schedule, the oldest dropped in the same write. Missed slots (DL-192's catch-up) are one `missed` entry with how many and from when.
- Off-boarding (DL-132) pauses every schedule of the person or service account it off-boards, first, before it cancels their jobs, with `paused_reason: owner removed` (`ScheduleOwnerRemoved`), and adds "N schedules paused" to its audit row when there were any. Deleting a schedule deletes its history; the jobs it created keep their `schedule_id` as a plain reference.
- Nothing is sent anywhere: a skip or failure is seen on the schedule (DL-195, DL-196).

## Rationale

Skipping keeps a slow job from stacking up runs, and the limit is the owner's own choice of spend. Pausing after three failures stops a schedule that can no longer work from failing every hour forever, while one transient failure does not stop it. The history is bounded so a schedule's row cost stays flat for its whole life.

## Consequences

- A schedule that is always still running when its next slot comes (an hourly schedule with a two-hour job) runs every other slot.
- A paused schedule stays paused until its owner resumes it, which recomputes `next_run_at` from now and resets the failure count.
