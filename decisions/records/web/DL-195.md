## Context

Schedules need a home in the browser: making one should feel like starting a job (DL-061), and seeing whether it is working must not need the CLI.

## Decision

- `/schedules/` lists the caller's schedules: name, cadence in words (`cadenceWords`: "Every hour", "Every day at 09:00 (Europe/Stockholm)", or the cron expression as written), state, next run, and the last fire's outcome with its job linked. It is reached from the jobs page's heading.
- `/schedules/new` and `/schedules/edit?ref=` (`ScheduleForm.svelte`) draw the job form's own fields, lifted out of `NewJob.svelte` into `JobFields.svelte` so both forms draw one copy, with a name and a cadence section: Hourly (its minute), Daily, Weekly, Monthly (each with its time, day of week or day of month 1 to 28) or Custom (a cron field), a time zone defaulting to the browser's (`Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone`), and the cadence in words under it (`cadenceWords`). No next runs are estimated in the browser: the server decides them, and the schedule's page shows its `next_runs` once saved. An edit sends what the form shows over the stored job (`scheduleJobOf`), so a field set from the CLI (variables, credentials, a landing, the PR policy) is kept and one the form shows and the person cleared is cleared; an edit's form is filled once from the first read.
- A schedule's page (`/schedules/view?ref=`, `ScheduleView.svelte`) shows its settings, its history with each job linked to its session page, its failure count and paused reason, and Run now, Pause or Resume, Edit and Delete; Delete and Run now sit behind `ConfirmAct`'s second press, as Cancel job does (DL-057).
- A job fired by a schedule says so: its row on `/jobs/` ("· from schedule <name>") and its session page's sidebar ("From schedule <name>", linked) show it, "(deleted)" once the schedule is gone. A run's job links to its session page by the session's name, `job-<id>`.
- On a phone the pages follow DL-106: the list's rows at four lines at most, the form in one column, the cadence's choices one control.

## Rationale

The job form is already the one place a job is described; a schedule is a job plus a cadence, so it reuses that form rather than growing a second one. Named cadences cover what most people want without reading cron; Custom keeps the rest possible.

## Consequences

- `web/src/lib/schedule.ts` carries the logic: building the cron expression from the named choices and back (`cronOf`, `cadenceOf`), the words for a cadence, what stops a save, the edit's job and the form's draft from a stored job (`draftOf`).
