## Context

Schedules need a home in the browser: making one should feel like starting a job (DL-061), and seeing whether it is working must not need the CLI.

## Decision

- `/schedules/` lists the caller's schedules: name, cadence in words (`cadenceWords`: "Every hour", "Every day at 09:00 (Europe/Stockholm)", or the cron expression as written), state, next run, and the last fire's outcome with its job linked. It is reached from the jobs page's heading and the header menu.
- `/schedules/new` and `/schedules/edit?ref=` reuse the job form's fields and logic (`jobform.ts`, `ModelPicker`, `ReasoningPicker`, `TemplatePicker`, the repository picker), without the sync kind and with Start replaced by Save, and add a cadence section: Hourly, Daily, Weekly, Monthly (each with its time, day of week or day of month) or Custom (a cron field), a time zone defaulting to the browser's (`Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone`), and the next three runs: while the form is edited, a local estimate (`cadencePreview` in `web/src/lib/schedule.ts`, labelled as an estimate); once saved, the server's `next_runs` from the view, which decides. No preview endpoint is added.
- A schedule's page (`/schedules/view?ref=`) shows its settings, its history with each job linked to its session page, its failure count and paused reason, and Run now, Pause or Resume, Edit and Delete; Delete and Run now sit behind `ConfirmAct`'s second press, as Cancel job does (DL-057).
- A job fired by a schedule says so: its row on `/jobs/` and its session page's sidebar show "From schedule <name>", linked.
- On a phone the pages follow DL-106: the list's rows at four lines at most, the form in one column, the cadence's choices one control.

## Rationale

The job form is already the one place a job is described; a schedule is a job plus a cadence, so it reuses that form rather than growing a second one. Named cadences cover what most people want without reading cron; Custom keeps the rest possible.

## Consequences

- `web/src/lib/schedule.ts` carries the cadence logic: building the cron expression from the named choices and back, and the local estimate of the next runs.
