## Context

On a phone the session page's slim bar, sticky at the top once the page's own header has scrolled away (DL-106), sometimes vanished in Firefox for Android while a session was live, and came back on the next scroll. It was not seen in Chrome, nor on a stopped session.

It was measured on a Pixel 9a in Firefox for Android, against a mocked running session that adds a turn every 20 seconds, with screenshots taken over USB and the bar detected by its "Running" text, while a probe on the page reported the bar's position:

- The bar was missing in 7 to 11 of 12 screenshots of an untouched page, and now and then while swiping. Every time, the page reported the bar sticky at `top: 0`: layout was right and Firefox did not draw it.
- It disappeared exactly when a turn grew the transcript and the page, following its end (DL-057), scrolled itself with an instant `scrollTo`, and it stayed missing until the reader's next touch scroll.
- Neither isolating the bar from the composer's height (moving `--dock-h` off its ancestor), promoting it to its own compositor layer (`translateZ(0)`), setting `scrollTop` directly, nudging its offset after the scroll, nor making it `position: fixed` changed this: a fixed bar vanished too.
- Following with a smooth scroll kept the bar drawn in every screenshot (0 of 24 and 0 of 32 runs, untouched and swiped), and the page still kept up with its end. Sending a message, whose return to the end stays instant, kept the bar too.

A live session is the only one whose transcript grows under the reader, which is why only it showed the fault.

## Decision

- The page's scrolls that follow a growing end are smooth: the one after a new block is drawn and the one on a resize of the body or the window (DL-154's keyboard follow), both through `followTo` (`behavior: 'smooth'`). They still run only while the tail latch is armed (DL-057).
- The return to the end after a send, and "Jump to latest", stay instant (`toEnd`, DL-057): a long smooth scroll from far up would pass through positions the latch reads.
- While the page follows, "Jump to latest" is not drawn: `atEnd` is `tail.following || atBottom`, since a following page is at its end or travelling there by a smooth scroll. A page that is not following is measured as before.
- Nothing else about the bar changes: it stays sticky, without a transform, and `--dock-h` stays where DL-106 put it.

## Rationale

The measurements point at the page's own instant scroll, not at the bar, its layer or its layout: every change to the bar left the fault, and only the kind of scroll removed it. A reader's own scroll, which Firefox runs itself, always brought the bar back, and Firefox runs a smooth programmatic scroll the same way; that is an inference from the behaviour, not from Gecko's source. Keeping the instant jump where DL-057 chose it keeps its latch reasoning intact, and the send path did not show the fault.

## Consequences

- Following is a short animation on every screen, not only a phone.
- A browser or setting that turns smooth scrolling off (a reduce-motion preference, Firefox's own smooth-scroll setting) makes the follow instant again and may bring the fault back on Firefox for Android; that was not tested.
- `sessiondetail.svelte.test.ts` checks that a growing transcript is followed with a smooth scroll and offers no "Jump to latest" meanwhile; jsdom cannot show the painting itself, which only a phone check can.
