## Context

On a phone the session page's slim bar, sticky at the top once the page's own header has scrolled away (DL-106), sometimes vanished in Firefox for Android while a session was live, and came back on the next scroll. It was not seen in Chrome, nor on a stopped session.

It was measured on a Pixel 9a in Firefox for Android, against a mocked running session that adds a turn every 20 seconds, with screenshots taken over USB and the bar detected by its "Running" text, while a probe on the page reported the bar's position:

- The bar was missing in 7 to 11 of 12 screenshots of an untouched page, and now and then while swiping. Every time, the page reported the bar sticky at `top: 0`: layout was right and Firefox did not draw it.
- It disappeared exactly when a turn grew the transcript and the page, following its end (DL-057), scrolled itself with an instant `scrollTo`, and it stayed missing until the reader's next touch scroll.
- Neither isolating the bar from the composer's height (moving `--dock-h` off its ancestor), promoting it to its own compositor layer (`translateZ(0)`), setting `scrollTop` directly, nudging its offset after the scroll, nor making it `position: fixed` changed this: a fixed bar vanished too.
- Following with a smooth scroll kept the bar drawn in every screenshot (0 of 24 and 0 of 32 runs, untouched and swiped), and the page still kept up with its end.
- With only the following made smooth, scrolling up and pressing "Jump to latest", whose return to the end was still instant, flickered the bar and once left it missing, seen by hand on the same phone.

A live session is the only one whose transcript grows under the reader, which is why only it showed the fault.

## Decision

- Every scroll the page makes is smooth, through `followTo` (`behavior: 'smooth'`): the one after a new block is drawn and the one on a resize of the body or the window (DL-154's keyboard follow), which still run only while the tail latch is armed, and the return to the end after a send and from "Jump to latest" (`toEnd`). This replaces DL-057's instant jump for the way back.
- The way back re-arms the latch before it scrolls, and the latch is released only by a move up while the content did not shrink (DL-070), so the frames of a smooth scroll down keep it armed. DL-057's reason for jumping instantly, that the frames of a smooth scroll released the latch, predates that rule.
- While the page follows, "Jump to latest" is not drawn: `atEnd` is `tail.following || atBottom`, since a following page is at its end or travelling there by a smooth scroll. A page that is not following is measured as before.
- Nothing else about the bar changes: it stays sticky, without a transform, and `--dock-h` stays where DL-106 put it.

## Rationale

The measurements point at the page's own instant scroll, not at the bar, its layer or its layout: every change to the bar left the fault, and only the kind of scroll removed it, wherever the page scrolled. A reader's own scroll, which Firefox runs itself, always brought the bar back, and Firefox runs a smooth programmatic scroll the same way; that is an inference from the behaviour, not from Gecko's source.

## Consequences

- Following, and the way back to the end, are a short animation on every screen, not only a phone; a long way back takes a moment rather than being instant.
- A browser or setting that turns smooth scrolling off (a reduce-motion preference, Firefox's own smooth-scroll setting) makes the follow instant again and may bring the fault back on Firefox for Android; that was not tested.
- `sessiondetail.svelte.test.ts` checks that a growing transcript is followed with a smooth scroll and offers no "Jump to latest" meanwhile, and that a send returns to the end with one; `tail.test.ts` checks that a smooth way back keeps the latch through every frame. jsdom cannot show the painting itself, which only a phone check can.
