## Context

DL-106 docked the composer to the foot of a phone's screen as a one-line field that grows with its text, a round Send beside it, and one chip showing the model on a row of its own. Used on a phone, it spends its space badly. At 390 px with a job running, the dock is about 130 px in three rows:

- the status bar (DL-059), with a full-size bordered Interrupt button;
- a bordered field of about 305 px next to a 44 px Send circle, which with its gap takes most of what is left of the width;
- a row holding only the model chip.

The field also stops growing at 128 px, about five lines, so a longer message scrolls inside a small box.

Chat apps on phones (Claude's own app is the one compared) put the text and its controls in one rounded container, with the text at full width and the controls in a row under it, and fold to a single row while nothing is being written. The aim is the same use of space, not their look.

## Decision

Below `md` only, except for Send's line (the last point), which goes at every width. From `md` up the composer is otherwise unchanged.

- **One container.** The text and a toolbar row share one rounded surface. The textarea has no border of its own and takes the container's full width.
- **The toolbar row**, inside the container under the text:
  - the model chip on the left, which opens what a phone already folds away (the model switch, and the "then continue / hand over" choice on a running job with its note);
  - Send on the right as a small filled circle (36 px drawn, with a 44 px tap area from a pseudo-element), drawn dimmed while the box is empty or a send is refused, as today's disabled state. The chip is 32 px drawn with the same 44 px tap area.
  - The container has a field's colour (`bg-raised`) in the light theme, where the page's own colour read as darker than the dock around it, and the page's colour (`bg-paper`) in the dark theme, where it reads as inset. The olive border is its focus mark in place of the field's outline.
  - The chip's own row is gone.
- **Folded while idle.** With the box empty, unfocused and the options closed, the container is one row: the placeholder and Send. Focusing the box, or any text in it, opens the toolbar row. Text never hides the toolbar, and the toolbar never closes while the options are open.
  - Focus is tracked on the form (`focusin`/`focusout`): the toolbar folds only when focus leaves the form, so moving between the box, the chip and the options' own controls keeps it open. A send keeps it open too, because it disables the box and a disabled box loses focus.
  - A press of the chip or Send does not take focus from the box (`mousedown` is cancelled). On a touch screen the box would otherwise blur at the press, the toolbar would fold, and the click would land on what moved under the finger.
  - The form carries `data-toolbar="open|folded"`, which is the state the stylesheet and the tests read. The chip and Send are grid cells of the form, not a wrapper of their own: Send sits beside the box while folded and beside the chip when open, so each control is drawn once.
- **A slimmer status line.** The state (`composerStatus`) stays above the container as one quiet line. Interrupt and Resume become compact text buttons on that line, drawn small with 44 px tap areas (a 44 px box with a negative vertical margin, so the line stays one text line tall), and the (i) stays as it is. They stay outside the form, as DL-059 requires, so Interrupt is reachable when sending is refused (a blocked job).
- **Taller growth.** The field grows with its text up to 40% of the dynamic viewport height (`dvh`) before it scrolls, instead of 128 px. The existing script sets the height to the text's; the stylesheet's `max-height` is what stops it, so there is one mechanism.
- **No line under Send, at any width.** DL-059's short line ("Read once the current step finishes; nothing is stopped.") and its (i) are dropped: the line cost a row on every screen to say what most readers never need. `deliveryNote` still explains what follows a message on a running job, behind an (i) beside the "then continue / hand over" choice it is about. An interactive session, and a job not offering the choice, have no such note.
- Everything else from DL-106 and DL-059 holds: Enter on a touch screen writes a new line and only Send (or Ctrl/Cmd-Enter) sends; a send re-arms the tail latch; the composer's options are one wrapper that a wider screen lays out with `md:contents`, never a second copy of any control.

## Rationale

- The text is what the reader is there to write; the container gives it the whole width, and moving Send and the chip into one shared row removes a row and a column at once.
- Folding to one row while idle returns the space to the transcript when the reader is reading, which on a phone is most of the time.
- Interrupt must stay outside the form (DL-059), so it cannot move into the toolbar; a slimmer line keeps it where it is without the bar's height.

## Consequences

- `Composer.svelte`'s phone markup changes: the form's grid becomes the container, the chip moves into the toolbar row, and the status pane gets compact buttons below `md`. Tests assert markup state (the toolbar's presence by focus and text, the chip's `aria-expanded`, Send's disabled state, Interrupt outside the form), since jsdom has no layout (DL-106).
- The tail latch's `ResizeObserver` (DL-106) already follows the dock changing height as the container folds and unfolds.
- The layout is looked at in a browser at 390 px (idle, several lines typed, options open, a running job with Interrupt) and at 768 and 1280 px to confirm nothing changed there.
