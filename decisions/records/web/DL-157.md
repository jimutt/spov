## Context

The page at `/tokens/` listed the caller's spov CLI tokens (DL-006) under the heading "Tokens" and the line "Tokens that can act as you". spov also stores many other secrets for a person: model provider keys, forge tokens, routed credentials, the OpenCode sign-in, all on the Settings page. A page named "Tokens" in the header reads as the place for those, and its line did not say which tokens it meant.

## Decision

- The page, its header link and its document title are called **Access tokens**. Its address stays `/tokens/`.
- Its lead says what they are: the reader's sign-ins to this spov server, one per terminal that ran `spov login`, which revoking signs out. It says the keys sessions use to reach a model provider or a git forge are not here, and links to Settings' Credentials section (`/settings/#credentials`, an anchor on that heading).
- The Credentials section's lead says it holds the keys and tokens sessions use for a forge or a model provider, and links back to Access tokens for the reader's own sign-ins.
- `docs/server.md` names the page the same way.

## Rationale

"Access token" is the usual name for a token that signs an account in, and saying in each place what the other holds settles the confusion where a reader meets it. The address is kept so bookmarks and the CLI's hints still work.

## Consequences

The header's link is wider; checked at 768 px and 1280 px that the header still fits on one line.
