## Context

On a phone the keyboard dropped and came back on every send. The composer disabled its box while a send was in flight, a disabled element loses focus, and the page focused it again when the send settled, so the keyboard closed and reopened. "Jump to latest" took the focus as well when pressed, which closed the keyboard. Nothing closed the keyboard when the reader dragged the conversation to read back, which a chat app does. A native chat app keeps the box focused and the keyboard up across a send and a jump, and closes it when the reader scrolls the messages.

## Decision

- A send clears the box at once and never disables it (`aria-busy` while in flight instead). The box keeps its focus, so a phone keeps its keyboard, and the page no longer refocuses the box when the send settles. A second send is still refused while one is in flight by the handler's own `sending` guard. This replaces DL-059's "cleared on success only": a refused or failed send puts its words back, ahead of anything written since it was cleared (`restoredDraft` in `web/src/lib/steer.ts`), so no words are lost; the echo shows them in the transcript meanwhile.
- "Jump to latest" cancels its `mousedown`, as the composer's own toolbar buttons do (DL-117), so a press takes no focus from the box and the keyboard stays up while the page jumps to the end.
- A touch that starts outside the dock while the box has focus, and drags `DismissDistance` (10 px) vertically, blurs the box, which closes a phone's keyboard (`dragDismisses`). Only a single finger counts; a drag inside the dock (the box itself, its toolbar) and a scroll the page makes itself never do. A mouse or a keyboard never blurs it.
- When the keyboard opens while the page follows the end, the page keeps the newest message above the dock (DL-154's resize follow); a reader scrolled up is left where they are.

## Rationale

These are the behaviours of a phone's own chat apps, and each one the page lacked made the keyboard jump or hid the newest message. Clearing at once is what those apps do, and putting the words back on a refusal keeps DL-059's purpose: a refusal never loses what the reader wrote.

## Consequences

- The box can be written into while a send is in flight; what is written then is kept behind the words a refusal returns.
- Mobile browsers do not let a page open or hold the keyboard by script; this only stops the page from closing it. On iOS Safari a press outside an editable field may still close it.
- jsdom has no keyboard and no layout, so the tests assert focus, the box's value and the helper functions; the keyboard itself is checked on a phone.
