## Context

`spov fly` takes seconds to tens of seconds to launch a sandbox, and then hands the terminal to OpenCode's own TUI. Plain progress lines before that make spov look like a script that precedes a real application, and they hide how long each phase took. A sandbox that takes 40 s to start instead of 4 s is the user's cost, and per-step timings are where it becomes visible.

The charm libraries the repository picker uses (`bubbles/v2`, `lipgloss/v2`) already cover a spinner and styled layout. The launch is driven by the server: `fly` creates the session over the API and follows the server's SSE step stream (DL-002), then connects through the loopback proxy, checks health and checks the version gates before the TUI starts.

## Decision

### The launch view

When stderr is a terminal at least as large as the view, `spov fly` draws a full-screen launch view on the alternate screen: an ASCII curlew (the storspov the tool is named after), a step list with a per-step timing, and a spinner on the active step. It never appears otherwise:

- A piped or redirected run keeps plain progress lines. Alternate-screen escape codes in a log are noise, and `fly --no-attach` must stay scriptable.
- `--plain` forces the plain lines on a terminal, for a screen reader or a terminal that draws the art badly.
- A terminal smaller than the view (54 columns by 25 rows, `viewWidth` and `viewHeight`) also gets plain lines, so a 24-row terminal does.

`Env.launchView` makes this decision and returns nil to keep the plain lines. The view is centred in a larger terminal; each row is padded on its own, because styled strings carry ANSI escapes, and the vertical padding uses the view's declared height, so the art does not move when the ground row fills in. A resize during the launch keeps the original centring. One 100 ms tick drives the spinner and the background flock of small birds that crosses the terminal.

### The steps are what spov can observe

The steps are `preparing tokens`, `starting sandbox` and `waiting for server`, declared in `session.LaunchSteps`. Cloning is deliberately not a step: it runs in the sandbox entrypoint, and the readiness probe only passes once the server is listening, which is after the clones finish. A cloning step would be invented, and would appear to complete at a moment spov cannot see.

Progress reporting is an interface, `session.StepReporter` (`Start`, `Done`, `Note`), not an `io.Writer`, so the launcher does not know whether steps are drawn or printed. `launchSession` feeds the reporter from the server's step events. `Note` carries text outside the sequence, such as a remote error.

### Fit

A view that does not fit the window is worse than the plain lines, so the view is sized for a normal terminal: 54 columns wide, 24 rows for the art and its status, 25 with the ground row under them.

- The art is a 39 by 24 derived asset (`art/spov.txt`), generated from the full-size drawing (`art/spov-source.txt`) by `internal/cli/art/shrink.py`, which maps the density of each 2x2 block to four ink levels (` . * @`). A replacement drawing is re-derived with the generator, not hand-shrunk, and the art is embedded in the binary, not read from config.
- The status block is 28 columns by 8 rows at column 26, row 15, bottom right beside the bird's legs. The whole view is 54 by 24, or 25 rows with the ground row under the art.
- The renderer emits exactly `artHeight` rows for an empty, partial or full status list, so the view never grows with its contents.
- `artRegionIsClear` (guarded by `TestArtRegionIsClear`) fails the build when the status block plus a clear gutter on every side meets any ink. New art means re-measuring the block, not trusting the constants.
- Status text is bounded by the block. `session.MaxStepNameWidth` (20) is checked where the names are declared, so a step name plus a marker and a right-aligned timing fits. Longer text is truncated by display cells, and sub-second timings are shown in seconds to fit.
- The view has one pose. A second pose, such as a takeoff when the sandbox is ready, would set the height of the shared box by whichever drawing is taller (a flying pose measured 55 rows) and must not be added without solving that.

### Teardown and terminal writes

The alternate screen is a global side effect, so the view is closed on every path out of a launch, failure and Ctrl-C included. `runFly` defers `Close` immediately after `Open`, and `Close` is idempotent. Terminal writes are serialised under a lock separate from the state lock, so a status update never waits on a slow terminal, and the `stopped` flag is re-checked inside the write lock, so a frame already in flight when `Close` runs discards itself and cannot paint over the restored shell.

Three rules keep the layout correct:

- Width is measured with `lipgloss.Width`, not by counting runes, because ANSI escapes occupy no cells.
- Truncation cuts by display cells, because a wide rune would otherwise overrun the block. `Note` carries remote error text, so this is reachable.
- Stripping style removes OSC hyperlinks as well as SGR sequences, or a hyperlink's URL spills into visible text.

### Handover to OpenCode

The view closes immediately before OpenCode takes the terminal, so the alternate screen gives way to OpenCode's own and not to a bare shell.

1. `prepareTUI` runs while the view is still up. It locates the local client and checks both version gates, against the one pin the server publishes (graded as in DL-035). `spov attach` shares it through `attachTUI`.
2. `closeView` closes the view and flushes held warnings, then `handOver` starts the client.
3. The session summary (sandbox, repositories, working directory, cost, attach and stop hints) prints after the TUI exits, not before it starts. With `--no-attach` there is no handover, so it prints once the view has closed. `--keep` hands over like any run and prints the summary after the TUI exits, then detaches.

With `--web` there is no local client to find or version-check: the sandbox's own version is still checked under the view, which then closes before the browser opens. `--web` and `--no-attach` exclude each other.

`Env.deferWarning` holds a message while a view is active and `flushWarnings` prints it when the view closes, because output under the alternate screen is discarded. With no view active it prints at once, so nothing is swallowed. Every failure path closes the view before reporting, so an error is never written under the alternate screen. If connecting fails after the sandbox is up, the view closes, the summary prints and the sandbox is left running for `spov attach` to retry.

## Rationale

- Per-step timings are the point of the view: they show where the launch time goes. A view that only decorates would not be worth the alternate screen.
- Falling back to plain lines off a terminal keeps `fly --no-attach` usable in a script.
- Version checking under the view costs the user nothing, since they are already watching a progress display. A process spawn and a comparison are dead time either way, and moving them under the view removes a visible gap.
- The summary is more useful after the session than before it, which is when the attach and stop commands matter.
- Downscaling to 3 by 2 (16 rows) dissolves the form into texture, which is why the art is scaled by 2 by 2.

## Measurements

- Real-Modal launch with a 77 by 55 two-pose view: 62 frames drawn over a launch of about 4 s, with real per-step timings, the view closed on the ready signal and the terminal restored. A run under a pty narrower than the art fell back to plain lines.
- Real-Modal launch on a 30-row terminal with the 54 by 24 view: 55 frames, the whole view inside the window, zero bytes of art after the terminal was restored.
- A frame in flight after the restore: 3639 bytes of art landed after the restore sequence and erased scrollback before the write lock re-checked `stopped`; zero after.
- Status block width: `preparing credentials` with a two-digit timing needed 29 cells against a 28-cell block, which is why step names are short and bounded by `MaxStepNameWidth`.
- View size: 54 by 24 (25 with the ground row); art 39 by 24. A 77 by 55 view was clipped on ordinary terminals, which is why the view is half that size.

These were taken in a manual run against Modal. `go test ./internal/cli/ -run 'ArtRegion|View|Truncate|Summary|Warnings'` covers the layout rules without a sandbox.

## Consequences

- A failed launch restores the terminal because teardown is a defer, not a call at the end of the happy path.
- The art cannot be changed from config. A user-supplied drawing of arbitrary size would break the block.
- A new step needs a name within `MaxStepNameWidth` and a place in `LaunchSteps`.
- The remaining flicker between the view and OpenCode's first frame is OpenCode's own startup (process spawn, runtime boot, first frame), which spov cannot shorten.
