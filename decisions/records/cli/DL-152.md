## Context

DL-123 made `spov init`'s output plain lines with no colour: a numbered heading per step, a tick, a cross or a dash, the state's word beside it, and the step's detail, explanation and conversation as indented lines of one weight. On a terminal every kind of line reads alike, so a step's facts, what they mean, a fix that failed and the commands to run next are hard to tell apart, and a failed fix's error reads as one more line of detail. Long lines wrap at the terminal's edge to column 0.

## Decision

### When it is styled

Init's stdout is styled only when stdout is a terminal (`Env.stdoutIsTerminal`), `NO_COLOR` is unset or empty, and `TERM` is not `dumb`; colours are reduced to what the terminal declares (`TERM`, `COLORTERM`, through `colorprofile.Env`), and a terminal that declares none gets no styling. A question goes to stderr and is styled only while stderr is a terminal too. `--json` is unchanged and never styled.

### The same lines either way

Styling adds escape sequences and nothing else: with them removed, a styled run prints exactly the plain run's lines in the same order. Prose (a step's detail, its explanation, a fix's conversation, an error, the closing lines) is wrapped at 80 columns in both modes, or at the terminal's width when it is narrower (never below 40), each line continuing at its own indent, never at column 0. A backtick span and a command are never broken; a word longer than the line is left whole. A line meant to be copied (a command, a line of a file) is never wrapped.

### The layout

- A step's heading is its mark, its number, its title and the state's word: `✓ 1. Modal  ok`. The word stays, as DL-123 says. The mark is green for ok, red for a missing required step, amber for a missing optional one (`not found`), and faint for waiting; the number and the word are dim and the title is bold.
- Under the title, at column 5: the step's facts dim, what it means and the fix's conversation in the terminal's own colour, a failed fix as `error:` in bold red followed by its message in red, continuing past the label. A line to copy sits at column 7.
- In prose, a backtick span, a path, an http or https address and an environment variable's name are drawn in the code colour.
- A question starts with `?` in amber, the question bold and `[y/N]` dim; a choice's options sit under it at column 7, numbered in amber.
- The closing block is `Ready.` in bold green, followed for each way to launch by a dim line saying what it does and the command on a line of its own at column 4; or `Not ready yet.` in bold red, a `-` line per missing step with its title bold and each command of its fix in the code colour, and a dim last line.
- No boxes, rules or emoji; the marks are the only symbols.

### The palette

The colours are the CLI's shared tones (`internal/cli/palette.go`): the launch view's and the repository picker's green, amber, dim and faint, with a red and a code blue added, each with a light and a dark value chosen by the background `COLORFGBG` names (dark by default).

## Rationale

Colour, weight and indent separate what a reader looks for on a terminal; keeping the text identical keeps a pipe, a CI log, `--check` and a pasted issue readable and greppable, and lets a test hold the two modes together. Wrapping in both modes is what makes the lines the same; 80 columns is a comfortable measure on a wide terminal, and following a narrower one keeps the indent.

## Consequences

- A new line init prints goes through `initStyle`, in a role, so it is styled and wrapped like the rest.
- Output that other commands print during a fix (storing a credential, the image build) keeps its own form.
- `TestInitIsStyledOnATerminalWithTheSameText` compares a styled and a plain run line for line; `TestInitIsPlainWithoutAColourTerminal` holds a pipe, `NO_COLOR`, `TERM=dumb` and an unset `TERM` to no escape sequence.
